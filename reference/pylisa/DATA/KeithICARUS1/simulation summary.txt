One run of each of Keith's four analogies with CompteSimilarity turned on.

The goal is to see whether we can get Keith's intuited pattern of effects using LISA's analog similarity 
routines as a basis for deciding whether the analogy is good or not.

To do so: Look at the analog similarities computed in an unbiased way (with role and filler similarity both
contributing to analog similarity) as compared to role-biased similarity, in which role but not filler
similarity contributes to analog similarity.

Keith's analogies:

(1) blizzard : snowflake :: army : soldier   (cross-domain, slow "true")
(2) blizzard : snowflake :: monsoon : raindrop (within-domain, fast "true")
(3) blizzard : snowflake :: tornado : cloud (false)
(4) blizzard : snowflake :: truck : vehicle (far false)

Analogy     Unbiased Similarity     Role-biased Similarity      Difference (biased - unbiased)
(1)             0.558                   0.714                       0.156
(2)             1.220                   1.260                       0.040
(3)             0.610                   0.637                       0.027
(4)             0.411                   0.515                       -0.104

Results are not very promising so far... nothing obvious leaping out Except that the rank-ordering of the role-biased
similarities follows the right ordinal pattern.  We'd just need to assume a threshold somewhere between 0.637 and 0.714.

Perhaps the role similarities in the non-analogy case need to be lower.  Also, perhaps I need to be looking, in the role-biased
similarity case, at the similarities of the roles *alone* (the biased results above took object similarity out of the 
equation but left SP and P similarity in, which basically rewards a good structura mapping even if roles don't map).
